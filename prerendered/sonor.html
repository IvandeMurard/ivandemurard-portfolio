    The Curiosity

    How do we reclaim silence in a world that's getting louder? We started with a simple question: "Can open-source data be the key to designing quieter, more livable cities?"

    The Observation

    Urban noise is the second largest source of urban nuisance in Europe. 25 million people are exposed to dangerous nighttime noise levels.

    It generates multiple deep disorders (chronic stress, cardiovascular diseases, cognitive impacts in children) and carries a heavy social cost (48 000 deaths/year in Europe). Yet noise remains largely invisible and under-addressed by public policies.

    The Hypothesis

    Our first 2020 prototype

    By making noise visible and data truly actionable (through real-time multi-source mapping, prioritized action plans, citizen engagement, and impact measurement), we could equip municipalities and urban planners with the tools to anticipate, act, and protect the most fragile resource of a city: silence.

    The Product

    From prototype to platform.

    Data Synthesis

    We designed and shipped a SaaS decision-support platform that aggregates and normalizes multiple open data sources, delivering high-fidelity, real-time city-wide acoustic mapping and actionable recommendations for urban planners.

    Citizen Engagement & Urban Quietude

    The platform puts residents at the center: citizens can report local issues and share testimonies directly. Planners receive prioritized action plans and can measure real impact over time, turning data into concrete public policies that protect acoustic health.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Why did you stop the project?

    The B2G sales cycles proved too long for an early-stage startup without significant bridging capital. While the pilot phase was successful, the timeline to secure recurring municipal contracts misaligned with our runway requirements.

    How did you fund it?

    We were supported by early grants (Bourse French Tech), incubation resources from Matrice, and a strategic prototype commission from Banque des Territoires, totaling approximately €20k in non-dilutive funding alongside sweat equity.

    What were the key technical challenges?

    Normalizing heterogeneous open-data sources, estimating noise propagation in complex urban canyons without dense IoT sensor networks, and building performant map visualizations that remained responsive with hundreds of thousands of data points.

    Acknowledgments

    This project would not have been possible without the support of Banque des Territoires and Matrice association, and the collaboration of my three co-founders: Émilie, Majda and Benjamin.
