    02The problem

    The problem

    What a hotel knows about its own service lives in a manager's head, and leaves with them.

    The bottleneck is not data, it is synthesis: combining occupancy and sales history with weather and local events into one recommendation for this property.

    The result, day to day:

    • Persistent over or under-staffing
    • Avoidable food waste
    • Reactive decision-making instead of anticipation
    • Slow onboarding and inconsistent execution across properties
    03Product strategy

    Product strategy

    Understand a property the way its manager does, and measure every recommendation against what happened.

    Five decisions follow from that.

    #DecisionWhy
    1Distribution before designA hotel floor has no time for a new tool. So the product plugs into the PMS and is built to be listed where hotels already pick their integrations
    2F&B firstA covers forecast has a result the next morning. It can be checked
    3WhatsApp as the only surfaceIt shows the recommendation, keeps a human line to the manager, and records the reply the agent learns from
    4Three parties, not one customerThe F&B manager uses it. Hotel management pays. The PMS distributes
    5Pricing on results, to startWith no track record, the builder carries the adoption risk. The later model is undecided

    Unproven

    • No hotel or PMS has validated this route yet.
    • The platform could build the same thing.
    • Pricing on labor cost needs payroll data the PMS does not hold.

    05What it does today

    What it does today

    One output runs daily. Two are hypotheses a pilot would test.

    Already working

    • States its drivers the evening before, then reports its own miss the next morning, in plain language.
    • Flagged a corrupted POS export rather than training on it.
    • Named its own drift after three consecutive misses, and recovered from a 25% regime shift through weekly recalibration.
    • Stores every recommendation next to what actually happened, with the manager's override recorded beside it.
    • Blocks its own merges on evaluation exit codes; every guardrail trip carries a machine-readable reason.

    Sandbox data, real mechanics, deterministic reruns.

    Three outputs

    OutputState
    Tomorrow's covers, with confidence and reasonsRunning daily on sandbox data
    Staffing that fits, approved or overridden by the managerPilot hypothesis
    Less waste, by flagging over-ordering before purchaseTarget outcome
    06Where it stands

    Where it stands

    The loop is built and testable. The forecast is not yet proven on hotels.

    Hotels connectedNone. No pilot partner signed, no go-live date
    Forecast testPublic data, 30 restaurants. Beats a simple "same weekday last week" guess on average. Matches it on a typical day. Slightly behind it for next-day forecasts, the horizon the product uses
    Limit of that testRestaurants, not hotels. No occupancy data, the signal the product relies on
    What is solid todayThe loop: recommendation, reply, outcome, and the checks that guard them

    Product vision

    Today each hotel learns from its own history. The longer-term idea is that independent hotels learn from each other through HiveMemory, a layer of shared anonymised patterns, and get the reach of a group without joining one.

    Anima, the guest memory node, has its own case study.

    Built to be trusted

    verified

    Evaluation as a contract

    Qonto-inspired. Versioned golden datasets. Any change to forecasting, providers, memory or prompts needs a green eval pipeline to merge. A hard CI gate, not a habit.

    monitoring

    Observability & metering

    Every MCP tool call is one structured event. Auditable, queryable, priceable. The product is operable, not opaque.

    lock

    Multi-tenant security

    hotel_id scoped at every layer (RLS, Python guards, MCP token binding), resolved server-side, never a tool input. Read-only PMS access. GDPR: Supabase EU, HiveMemory is anonymized aggregates only.
