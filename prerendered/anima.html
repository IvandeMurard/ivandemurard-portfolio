    Anima

    Memory that means no guest has to ask twice.

    Keeps what a guest said and what happened, so nobody asks twice, without watching.

    How the nodes fit together: the architecture note

    Sec. 01

    The questions it exists to answerlink

    keySection 01

    Four questions a general manager cannot answer today, and every one of them is a memory question.

    In practiceA guest asked for a quiet room on three consecutive stays, and had to ask again on the fourth.

    • What does this property know about a guest that nobody ever wrote down?
    • What did we learn last stay that we will fail to use during this one?
    • What does a guest have to repeat every single time, and what does that cost us?
    • Which of the things we believe about them did they tell us, and which did we infer?

    The fourth one separates this from a customer database. A property that cannot tell what a guest said from what a system inferred has already lost the argument it will eventually have with the guest, or with a regulator.

    Sec. 02

    Where it stands, and what the gate is forlink

    keySection 02

    A synthetic proof of concept today, with the data-protection assessment placed before the first real guest datum.

    In practiceIt has never run on a real guest, and it will not until that assessment is complete.

    An employee can be told they are being modelled. A guest cannot meaningfully consent to being modelled during a two-night stay.

    The older version of this was the concierge who never had to ask twice: a guest memory running on one person's recall, with a bus factor of one. So Anima is a guest memory, not a twin.

    Sec. 03

    One stay, four memory layerslink

    keySection 03

    Four layers with different lifetimes, because permanence is the default failure.

    In practiceA guest orders a decaf at 11pm before an early flight. Tonight, that matters. Forever, it is a false belief.

    The four memory layers of Anima, what each holds and how long it lives
    LayerHoldsLifetime
    WorkingThe current stay, in progressExpires at check-out
    EpisodicThis stay and a short tail after itWeeks
    SemanticDurable preferences, stated or repeatedly confirmedLong, with decay
    SegmentAnonymised patterns, what guests like this tend to needAggregate, never individual

    Working memory should use the decaf tonight, so the bar does not ask twice. Semantic memory should never conclude the guest drinks decaf.

    Sec. 04

    Claims carry confidence, and confidence decayslink

    keySection 04

    Nothing is stored as a fact. Everything is a claim with a source, a weight and an age.

    In practiceA preference from 2019 that nobody confirmed since does not outvote something said last week.

    Weights fall over time unless something renews them. A system that reports a confidence it did not measure is worse than one that reports none.

    Sec. 05

    Remembering is not neutrallink

    keySection 05

    The stay no longer ends at check-out. Whether to remember is an act of care or an exercise of power.

    In practiceThe guest leaves the building and their record stays, in infrastructure that never forgets and never forgives.

    Whether to remember is an act of care or an exercise of power.

    Edmondo Grassi, The Remains of the Stay: Memory, Identity, and the Afterlife of the Guest, Hotel Yearbook 2026 Technology Edition.

    Sec. 06

    Scope-gated, and it never decideslink

    keySection 06

    Every consumer declares which layer it is querying, and no call returns a whole person.

    In practiceThe orchestrator asks for semantic-layer preferences relevant to a room assignment, and nothing else.

    Anima answers one question, which is who this guest is right now. What to do about it belongs to the orchestrator, with a human validating, the same separation between perception and decision the F&B node enforces.

    Sec. 07

    Where it sits in the architecturelink

    keySection 07

    One of four domain nodes, five agents with the orchestrator.

    In practiceGuest experience is not a node: guest.* lives in the shared contract, not behind this one service.

    What Anima knows reaches other nodes only through the orchestrator, one layer at a time. Guest experience itself is not a node, which is why guest.* lives in the shared contract rather than behind this one service.

    The architecture note has the whole picture, including what the other nodes own and what they refuse.
